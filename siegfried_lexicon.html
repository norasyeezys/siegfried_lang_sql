Pwgc ain Daughter forms by century-ish: Gothic ains (Wulfila, 4th c.) — diphthong intact Old Norse einn (n-assimilation later) Old English ān — monophthongized Old Saxon ēn — monophthongized Old Frisian ēn / ān — monophthongized Old High German ein — diphthong preserved Wachtendonck ēin (9th–10th c. Ripuarian Frankish) — mixed/transitional spelling, the long ē showing the monophthongization wave reaching Ripuarian from the Ingvaeonic side, the residual -i- preserving the older diphthongal coloring. Exactly what you'd expect from Central Franconian sitting on the Low/High German hinge. Siegfried's window is ~420 AD. Wachtendonck is 400–500 years downstream. The monophthongization that produced ēin hadn't happened yet, or was barely beginning. The strongest contemporary anchor is Gothic ains — Wulfila's translation is mid-4th c., one generation before Siegfried, and a different branch of Germanic preserving the diphthong tells you the diphthong was the live form across the whole family in that window. OHG ein on the adjacent dialect continuum confirms it from the other side: High German never monophthongized this vowel at all, so its preservation there is the strongest signal that the diphthong was the original Frankish form before regional drift split things.

frisk

/'frisk/

fresh
OHGfriscOEfersċONferskr
Found in Wartburg as "germanic". Form /frisk/ is the inherited Ripuarian reflex of PG friska- "fresh, new, unspoiled, vigorous, cool." Distinct evidentiary status: the Romance loan stratum (It. fresco, Sard. friscu, Cat. fresc, Sp./Pg. fresco, Friul. fresk, OFr. freis > Mod.Fr. frais) descends from a West Germanic borrowing into Vulgar Latin, NOT from Frankish specifically. Donor cannot be identified as Frankish vs. Old Saxon vs. general continental West Germanic because all dialects had /frisk/ with /sk/ preserved at the contact date and no diagnostic features distinguish them. Dating: 3rd-5th c. AD. Sardinian friscu may date the loan before Sardinian's split from continental VL (3rd-4th c.), possibly predating Frankish individuation as a distinct dialect (Franks first attested ~257 AD as Lower Rhine federation). OFr. freis shows /sk/ palatalization, dating loan into Gaulish VL before 5th-7th c. palatalization wave. Geographic distribution (present in Italian/Iberian/Gallo-Romance/Sardinian, absent in Romanian and Dalmatian) confirms western diffusion in late Imperial / post-Imperial period. Therefore: Romance reflexes are SIBLINGS of the Ripuarian form, not descendants, and confirm /sk/ preservation in West Germanic at the contact period without making Ripuarian the donor. PIE etymology uncertain; cf. Lith. prėskas "tasteless, unleavened," OCS prěsnъ "fresh, unleavened," possibly from PIE *preisk-, but vowel and -sk- suffix correspondences are not clean (Kroonen 2013 acknowledges Balto-Slavic comparanda without committing to a reconstruction).

Spelled as germia in Wartburg. Surface form: */germja/, feminine ja-stem (West Germanic productive feminine class with the -ja- thematic suffix).
